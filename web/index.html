How the numbers are produced

From lake telemetry to a tactical rating.

This page describes the implemented rules: how we calculate Bite Score, estimate water temperature, rank species, and select fishing windows. These are condition heuristics, not calibrated catch probabilities.

01 · Bite Score algorithm

5–100 points

Start with a 35-point baseline. Add nine modifiers, apply any severe-drawdown ceiling, then round and bound the result.

raw = 35 + pressure + wind + clouds
         + solar + lunar + water temperature
         + dam flow + lake level + rainfall

if a drawdown ceiling applies:
    raw = min(raw, ceiling)

Bite Score = clamp(round(raw), 5, 100)
EPIC80–100
GOOD65–79
FAIR45–64
TOUGH5–44

Missing or non-finite pressure trend, wind speed, or cloud cover returns UNAVAILABLE. An unavailable optional modifier generally contributes zero; it is not treated as a measured zero. Water temperature may instead come from the estimate below.

Solar timing−10 to +15

PyEphem calculates sunrise, sunset, solar transit, and altitude at the lake’s latitude and longitude. Rules are checked in this order:

Solar conditionPoints
Within 90 minutes of sunrise or sunset+15
Otherwise, within 135 minutes of solar transit−10
Otherwise, Sun below −6° altitude+5
All remaining times0

The astronomical observer uses a flat horizon and standard atmospheric refraction; local terrain is not modeled.

Lunar timing0 to +22

Let h be hours from the nearest lunar event. Major windows use upper/lower transit; minor windows use moonrise/moonset. Major takes precedence if windows overlap.

Major, h ≤ 1:    16 × (1 − h) + bonus
Minor, h ≤ 0.75: 10 × (1 − h / 0.75) + bonus
Outside windows: bonus only

The phase bonus is +6 within 0.06 of a lunar cycle from new/full moon, +3 within 0.12, and zero otherwise. Cycle position is computed between the preceding and next new moon. These weights have not been validated against catches.

Rainfall & dam flow−12 to +7 / +5

Current rainfall is the sum of four complete 15-minute intervals over one hour. The forecast uses hourly rainfall totals.

Reported dam release above 100 cfs adds +5. A release at or below 100 cfs, or an unavailable release, adds zero. This does not measure current velocity at your fishing spot.

02 · Water-temperature estimate

Fallback model · °F

A fresh measured water temperature is preferred. When none is available, the model combines a seasonal reservoir baseline with a smaller air-temperature influence.

d = day of year
seasonal = 67 + 21 × sin(2π × (d − 119) / 365.2425)
air = 0.75 × trailing_96h_air_mean + 0.25 × current_air
estimate = seasonal + 0.30 × (air − seasonal)
water = round(clamp(estimate, 35, 94), 1) °F

The seasonal baseline ranges roughly from 46°F to 88°F. Blending 70% seasonal baseline with 30% effective air temperature reduces the effect of one hot or cold afternoon. It remains a heuristic surface-temperature estimate; depth, mixing, water clarity, and reservoir-specific heat exchange are not modeled.

Input selection & fallbacks

The date normally comes from the reading or forecast timestamp. A month-only call uses the middle of that month. If the 96-hour air mean is unavailable, the effective-air calculation uses the current air temperature for both terms.

The helper defaults missing/invalid current air to 70°F. The current-analysis caller currently supplies 78°F when its air reading is absent; the forecast passes the air reading to the helper. These are assumed inputs, not measurements.

The dashboard marks the result as estimated. Historical charts can contain a mix of measured and estimated temperatures.

03 · Species ranking

Separate 0–100 scale

Only species listed by ODWC for the selected lake enter the ranking. Each begins at 50 points. Species-specific rules add or subtract points for known temperature, pool level, elevation trend, reported discharge, weather, and season.

species_score = clamp(50 + applicable_species_modifiers, 0, 100)
order = highest score first; alphabetical order breaks ties

Missing observations do not activate their rules. Listed species without a custom rule remain neutral candidates at 50. The complete factor list is available in Why these fish? on the dashboard.

Discharge-based species rules classify the larger available inflow/release: below 100 cfs = NONE, 100–199 = LIGHT, 200–799 = MODERATE, and 800 or above = STRONG. With neither measurement available, current is UNKNOWN.

Species scores rank modeled conditions; they do not estimate abundance, confirm spawning, or represent a likelihood of catching that species.

04 · Forecasts & fishing windows

Same Bite Score engine

Each future hour uses forecast pressure trend, wind, clouds, and hourly rainfall, plus its own solar/lunar timing. Reservoir temperature, release, pool difference, and elevation trend are held at the latest baseline rather than forecast independently.

window_average = mean(three consecutive hourly Bite Scores)
best = highest rounded window_average
       then highest peak score if averages tie

The search uses upcoming hours within the next 24 forecast entries. A candidate containing an unavailable score or an hourly gap is excluded. If fewer than three entries are available, the function can evaluate a shorter window. The peak-hour card is the strongest individual upcoming hour, separate from the best average window.

Pool differenceElevation − operating reference
24h pool changeCurrent − prior elevation
Pressure trendCurrent − three-hour prior pressure

The 24-hour pool comparison uses an available elevation reading 24–25 hours before the latest reading. Seasonal operating references are used where available, rather than treating every lake’s reference as constant year-round.

Weather observation expiry1 hour
CWMS observation expiry4 hours
Hefner / USGS observation expiry6 hours

Freshness is evaluated per metric from its source observation time. Stale or missing values stay unavailable instead of becoming artificial zeroes. A seasonal phase label summarizes broad lake conditions; it does not verify spawning for a species.

06 · Color coding

Reading the dashboard

Colors identify measurements, score bands, or direction of change. A measurement’s color alone does not mean good fishing, safe water, or a validated threshold. Labels, numbers, and units remain the source of detail.

Water & pool
Cyan

Elevation; unchanged 24-hour pool level.

Amber

Water temperature; falling 24-hour pool level; more than 1 ft below normal pool.

Green

Inflow; rising 24-hour pool level; pool within ±1 ft of its operating reference.

Blue

Dam release; more than 1 ft above normal pool.

Purple

Dissolved oxygen. No oxygen-quality threshold is encoded by this color.

Rising/falling colors follow the sign of the reported 24-hour change, even for small changes. The ±1 ft pool display band is a visual reference; the algorithm uses the separate scoring thresholds above. Estimated temperatures retain the explicit “Estimated” label.
