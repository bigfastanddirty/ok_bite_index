Live Bite Score Architecture

The Bite Index begins at a 35-point baseline, then adds or subtracts condition-specific modifiers. The final result is clamped to 5–100. The dashboard's expandable Why this score? panel exposes these same live contributions for the selected lake.

Solunar 0 to +22 pts

5. Lunar Transit & Phase

Major and minor lunar windows are calculated from approximate lunar longitude and local sidereal time. A moon-phase proximity bonus may be added near major lunar phases.

  • • Major transit window: up to +16
  • • Minor transit window: up to +10
  • • Lunar phase proximity bonus: +3 or +6

Score Assembly

Final Bite Score = clamp(35 + Pressure + Wind + Clouds + Time + Solunar + Water Temp + Flow + Pool Trend + Rain, 5, 100)

The factors are additive modifiers, not percentage weights. A strong positive condition can offset a negative condition, while the final clamp prevents scores below 5 or above 100.

Target Species Ranking

Target-species selection is a separate scoring system from the 0–100 Bite Index. The candidate list comes from ODWC's Fish Species of Interest for each lake. The model never introduces a target that is not in that lake's stored ODWC species list.

1. 50-Point Baseline

Every ODWC-listed species begins at 50 before current conditions are evaluated.

2. Species-Specific Modifiers

Water temperature, pool level/trend, current, pressure, wind, clouds, rain, and season add or subtract points according to species behavior.

3. Top Three Targets

Scores are capped at 0–100, sorted highest-first, and the top three become the current Primary Target Species.

Examples of condition matching
  • • Largemouth Bass: warm water, elevated/rising pool, moderate wind, falling pressure, cloud cover.
  • • Crappie: 52–72°F preferred, stable pool, manageable wind, spring/fall timing.
  • • Blue Catfish: warm water, active current, elevated/rising water, rainfall/runoff.
  • • White / Striped / Hybrid Bass: bait-concentrating wind and current, favorable temperature, falling pressure.
Additional species profiles
  • • Walleye / Saugeye / Sauger: cooler water, wind, low light, cool-season timing.
  • • Channel Catfish: warm water, rainfall, inflow, elevated pool, warm-season timing.
  • • Flathead Catfish: warm water, current edges, low light, warm-season timing.
  • • Smallmouth / Spotted Bass: moderate temperatures, wind, structure, current and seasonal movement.

Species scores are tactical confidence rankings, not population estimates or guarantees of catch success.

Estimated Water Temperature & Limnological Thermal Decay Model

Not every USACE reservoir or USGS gauge station maintains an active in-situ sub-surface thermistor year-round. When physical temperature telemetry is offline, OK Bite Index activates an automated Thermal Decay & Thermal Inertia Model:

1. Exponential Moving Average (EMA)

Calculates a 7-to-10-day weighted moving average of ambient air temperatures to capture lake thermal lag and heat capacity.

2. Solar Radiation & Inflow Modifiers

Factors shortwave solar radiation (W/m²), cloud cover opacity, and major cold runoff inflows from upstream tributaries.

3. Reservoir Mean Depth Damping

Applies reservoir-specific thermal damping coefficients based on mean pool depth (e.g., deep lakes like Tenkiller vs. shallow lakes like Canton).

*Whenever physical telemetry is reporting live, the estimated model is automatically bypassed in favor of raw sensor readings.

Dissolved Oxygen (O₂) Saturation & Limnological Estimation

Most freshwater reservoirs lack continuous electrochemical optical dissolved oxygen probes in shallow surface bays. When in-situ USGS DO telemetry is offline, OK Bite Index calculates theoretical surface DO saturation (mg/L) derived from the Henry's Law water-temperature solubility relationship:

1. Temperature-Dependent Solubility

Cold water holds substantially higher dissolved oxygen concentrations (DO ≈ 14.6 - 0.1 × Water Temp °F). As surface water warms above 80°F, saturation thresholds naturally decay toward 6.0–7.0 mg/L.

2. Wind Aeration & Inflow Dynamics

Surface mechanical agitation from sustained wind (>8 mph) and high-volume spillway/tailrace flows increase atmospheric re-aeration across upper epilimnion layers.

3. Hypolimnetic Anoxia Suppression

Identifies summer thermal stratification cutoff points (thermoclines) where deep bottom basins drop below 3.0 mg/L, concentrating active predators into shallow oxygen-rich margins.

*When active USGS optical DO sensors report live telemetry (e.g., Lake Arcadia or Skiatook), estimated values are bypassed and flagged as raw physical readings.
