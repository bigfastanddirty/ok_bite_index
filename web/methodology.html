Severe drawdown acts as a final score ceiling after all weather, solunar, temperature, flow and other modifiers are evaluated. This prevents favorable short-term conditions from masking severely abnormal reservoir conditions.

Score Assembly

Final Bite Score = clamp(35 + Pressure + Wind + Clouds + Time + Solunar + Water Temp + Flow + Lake Level + Rain, 5, 100)

The factors are additive modifiers, not percentage weights. A strong positive condition can offset a negative condition. The normal score is clamped between 5 and 100; however, severe reservoir drawdown can impose a lower maximum score after all other modifiers are evaluated.

Score Baselines

OK Bite Index uses two separate additive scoring systems. Each starts from a neutral baseline, then current environmental conditions add or subtract points.

Overall Bite Index Base 35

Every lake-wide Bite Score begins at 35 points. Pressure, wind, cloud cover, time of day, solunar conditions, water temperature, dam flow, lake level, and precipitation then modify that baseline.

Bite Score = 35 + live condition modifiers
Target Species Score Base 50

Every lake-listed species begins at 50 points. Species-specific temperature, hydrology, current, weather, light, and seasonal conditions then add or subtract points before the candidates are ranked.

Species Score = 50 + species-specific modifiers

The two scores are independent. A 70 Bite Index does not mean a species with a 70 Target Species Score was calculated from the lake-wide Bite Index.

Target Species Ranking

Target-species selection is a separate scoring system from the 0–100 Bite Index. Every candidate begins at a 50-point baseline, then species-specific conditions add or subtract points. The candidate list comes from ODWC's Fish Species of Interest for each lake, and the model never introduces a target that is not in that lake's stored ODWC species list.

1. 50-Point Baseline

Every ODWC-listed species begins at 50 before current conditions are evaluated.

2. Species-Specific Modifiers

Water temperature, pool level/trend, current, pressure, wind, clouds, rain, and season add or subtract points according to species behavior.

3. Top Three Targets

Scores are capped at 0–100, sorted highest-first, and the top three become the current Primary Target Species.

Examples of condition matching
  • • Largemouth Bass: warm water, elevated/rising pool, moderate wind, falling pressure, cloud cover.
  • • Crappie: 52–72°F preferred, stable pool, manageable wind, spring/fall timing.
  • • Blue Catfish: warm water, active current, elevated/rising water, rainfall/runoff.
  • • White / Striped / Hybrid Bass: bait-concentrating wind and current, favorable temperature, falling pressure.
Additional species profiles
  • • Walleye / Saugeye / Sauger: cooler water, wind, low light, cool-season timing.
  • • Channel Catfish: warm water, rainfall, inflow, elevated pool, warm-season timing.
  • • Flathead Catfish: warm water, current edges, low light, warm-season timing.
  • • Smallmouth / Spotted Bass: moderate temperatures, wind, structure, current and seasonal movement.

Species scores are tactical confidence rankings, not population estimates or guarantees of catch success.

Estimated Water Temperature & 96-Hour Thermal Inertia Model

When verified in-reservoir temperature telemetry is unavailable, OK Bite Index estimates surface-water temperature from a smooth Oklahoma seasonal reservoir cycle plus recent atmospheric forcing. The estimate is intentionally slow-moving so a single hot or cold hour cannot unrealistically move lake temperature.

1. Seasonal Reservoir Baseline

A continuous day-of-year curve represents the delayed annual warming and cooling cycle of Oklahoma reservoirs without abrupt month-boundary jumps.

2. Trailing 96-Hour Air Mean

Atmospheric forcing uses 75% trailing 96-hour mean air temperature and 25% current air temperature, damping short-lived weather spikes.

3. Conservative Thermal Response

Only 30% of the difference between effective air temperature and the seasonal reservoir baseline is applied, with broad 35–94°F physical sanity bounds.

*Verified in-reservoir sensor telemetry always overrides the estimator. River, tailrace, and upstream stream temperatures are not treated as lake temperature.

Dissolved Oxygen Saturation Estimate

When a measured dissolved-oxygen value is unavailable, the dashboard displays a surface oxygen-saturation estimate. It is a physical solubility proxy, not a prediction of actual oxygen concentration throughout the water column.

1. Temperature Solubility Curve

A freshwater oxygen-solubility polynomial converts the current lake water temperature to an approximate saturation concentration in mg/L.

2. Surface-Pressure Correction

The saturation estimate is adjusted by current local surface pressure relative to standard sea-level pressure.

3. Explicit Estimate Label

Estimated values are displayed as “sat est.” They do not model photosynthesis, biological oxygen demand, thermocline depth, or hypolimnetic depletion.

*If verified in-situ dissolved-oxygen telemetry is available in the API, the measured value is displayed instead of the saturation estimate.
